The choice in 2068

You should not have to leave the place you love to get ahead.

Ambrosia feeds Caprica. Its farms, small businesses and close communities are a national strength. Yet a broken water main, an unreliable road or a lack of nearby training can still put an ordinary ambition out of reach.

Caprica’s gross public debt now stands at 68% of GDP. The People’s Party believes stronger national finances must support opportunity people can see in their own towns. That is the case Alistair will make for Ambrosia in the next Parliament.

Clean water at home. More value from what we grow. A decent connection to work and care. A future here, for every generation.

01 / Water & public services

Turn on the tap. Trust what comes out.

Clean water is the first test of a government that works. A family should know its supply is safe, and a farmer should know who will fix a failing pipe.

Repair the worst problems first+

Seek a ten-year regional renewal agreement, starting with an independently assessed list of failing pipes and treatment systems. Prioritize contamination risks, schools, clinics and communities facing repeated interruptions. Publish costs and the authority responsible before work begins.

Make the results easy to find+

Publish water tests, repair schedules and interruption notices by district, online and through local service points. Give residents a named contact and a clear route to escalate an unresolved problem. National investment should come with visible local responsibility.

Protect the source as well as the supply+

Work with farmers on soil health, efficient irrigation and runoff control. Stewardship payments should buy measurable improvements in water quality and habitat, with practical advice and transparent results. Protect productive land while restoring rivers and catchments.

02 / Farms & local enterprise

Grow it here. Keep more of its value here.

Ambrosia produces the food. More of the processing, skilled work and business income should stay in the communities that grow it.

Back the next investment+

Connect farms and local firms to the Alliance’s proposed immediate expensing of qualifying equipment. Help businesses compare machinery, irrigation and storage investments on their merits. Support must be open to family firms, with clear eligibility and published costs.

Bring processing closer to the farm+

Use competitive regional agreements to address missing cold storage, food processing and freight connections. Require credible demand, private investment and an independent business case before committing public funds. Measure the additional value retained locally.

Open a route to new customers+

Bring national export certification, logistics and trade finance advice to Ambrosian producers through a regional service. Connect small exporters to the Alliance’s single customs window and Columbian market agreements, with clear fees and limits on public exposure.

03 / Roads & connections

The last few miles matter.

A new export agreement means little if a truck cannot reach the farm. Opportunity depends on the everyday connections between our villages, towns and western ports.

Repair the routes we rely on+

Publish a condition survey of roads, bridges and freight bottlenecks, then agree a funded maintenance schedule with regional authorities. Put safety, school access and reliable journeys to market first. Every major proposal must account for its running costs.

Make a reliable connection the standard+

Map broadband gaps and tender support for areas the market cannot serve alone. Contracts should specify usable speeds, reliability and remedies when service falls short. Keep assisted and telephone access to essential public services.

Make services reachable+

Work with operators on practical links between Auburne, Garrison, Strawberry, Meridian and nearby villages. Coordinate timetables around work, study and health appointments. Bring participating services into the national fare cap as agreements are reached.

04 / Homes, skills & community

Your next chapter can start at home.

Leaving Ambrosia should be a choice. Staying should offer a real chance at a home, a useful qualification and work with a future.

Homes with services from the start+

Seek growth agreements for Strawberry and Meridian that join housing to water, school places, transport and jobs. Support well-designed infill under the national planning deadlines, protecting flood safety and productive farmland. Back secure tenancies and portable deposits, with limited ownership help reserved for additional new homes rather than bidding up existing stock.

A qualification that leads somewhere+

Bring employers and technical colleges together around agriculture, food technology, engineering, health and tourism. Share apprenticeship administration so smaller businesses can take part. Connect the national work-and-learning offer to rural placements, alongside portable training accounts and recognized qualifications for career changes and a return to work.

Care and opportunity close to home+

Make primary care appointments, referral transport and visiting clinics work together, with a clear contact when a referral stalls. Support the national funded respite offer for unpaid caregivers. Develop tourism around Mount Ambrosia within habitat and water limits, creating work that helps sustain services throughout the year.

The standard for every regional agreement

A date. A name.
A public account.

BEFORE A COMMITMENT

Show the whole bill.

Publish the options, the responsible authority and the cost of construction and upkeep. Use open procurement and independent appraisal, with property rights and a route to appeal protected.

WHILE WORK IS UNDERWAY

Keep the next date visible.

Report milestones, contract changes and delays each quarter. Water tests, repair schedules and housing progress should show what is changing in each community, not just across the region.

BETWEEN ELECTIONS

Make representation reachable.

Hold rotating constituency meetings across our towns, with telephone appointments too. An MP cannot run every service. Alistair can pursue an answer, scrutinize spending and explain his votes.

Meet Alistair

Ambrosia shaped him.
Service brought him here.

Alistair grew up on the Foulke family estate in rural Ambrosia. He is the grandson of former President and Prime Minister Darjeeling Foulke, but the experience he brings to this campaign is also local: the rhythms of farm country and the long work of representing a community.

Born at St. Joseph’s Hospital in Auburne, Montiablo, he studied international relations at the University of Auburne. A pitcher and right fielder at school, he helped his baseball team reach the regional summer tournament quarterfinals and played a year at college before focusing on his degree.

He went on to work at the Foulke Institute and became active in the National Party, later joining Moderate Reform. He married Elizabeth McKeon in 2034, taking the surname Foulke-McKeon, and was first elected on the Ambrosia Country Liberal Party list in 2040.

In 2068, Alistair is the People’s Party candidate for Ambrosia within the Liberal-Conservative Alliance. He brings deep local experience to a shared national program, and a clear commitment to making rural voices count.
