01

Work that stays here

An open port. A stronger local economy.

Bring Port Aaron’s next phase into focus↓

Seek a published delivery review of the Prosper Myrati port program: deep-water berths, container handling, shipbuilding and repair facilities, and the mainland trade corridor. Separate completed work from funded commitments and proposals still awaiting approval. Prioritize the next investments by demand, local employment and whole-life cost.

A Free Trade Port with clear public rules↓

Pursue Free Trade Port status with simpler customs procedures, transparent eligibility and time-limited investment incentives. Retain labor, environmental and anti-smuggling standards. Publish the cost of concessions and review whether they produce additional investment and durable jobs rather than simply subsidizing activity that would happen anyway.

Give local firms a way into the work↓

Publish upcoming contracts in accessible lots where practical, offer supplier briefings and enforce prompt payment. Connect maritime employers with local training providers for paid apprenticeships. Small exporters should have access to customs advice, shared logistics and cold-chain facilities where a business case supports them.

02

An island within reach

Better journeys. More possibilities.

Finish the journey beyond the terminal↓

Coordinate port and airport investment with the roads, rail links and local services that connect them to communities. Publish a single capital plan with responsible agencies, dependencies and maintenance costs. A new terminal should not leave passengers or freight waiting at the gate for the next connection.

An airport built around real demand↓

Review the proposed second runway, cargo facilities and passenger expansion against updated traffic forecasts, safety requirements and environmental assessments. Sequence construction to meet demand and protect public finances. Encourage competing services and transparent airport charges rather than promising routes government cannot guarantee.

Make island travel dependable↓

Work with operators on coordinated timetables and clear disruption information. Where an essential connection cannot support itself commercially, use a transparent, competitively tendered service contract with measurable reliability standards. Include access to education and medical appointments in the assessment of public need.

03

A future to come home to

Learn widely. Build a life here.

Protect the scholarship pathway↓

Back the Prosper Myrati program’s provision for 1,200 annual student scholarships, covering academic, technical and professional study. Publish eligibility, selection criteria and annual award data. Assess travel and living costs alongside tuition so opportunity is not reserved for families who can already afford to leave.

Make returning a real choice↓

Build a voluntary placement network linking students and graduates with Myratian employers, schools, clinics and maritime firms. Offer paid work experience with published terms. Recognize relevant qualifications and connect returning professionals with local opportunities, without imposing punitive obligations on students who study away.

Homes and services that grow together↓

Pair port and airport growth with homes on suitable serviced sites near jobs and transport, using the Alliance’s regional housing agreements. Plan water, school and clinic capacity alongside construction. Seek a funded island care-access plan, including referral transport and visiting services, before activating national waiting-time guarantees. Report outstanding reconstruction needs separately, so existing residents can see when their homes and services will be restored.

04

A Myrati for all of us

Our identity. Everyone’s dignity.

Keep decisions close to the island↓

Defend Myrati’s language, schools and regional institutions. Seek clear agreements giving regional authorities practical control over local tourism and transport, with funding responsibilities spelled out. National investment should strengthen local capacity, not make every decision depend on a distant office.

The same fair rules for everyone↓

Support community policing, independent complaints handling and due process. Protect peaceful assembly and political disagreement. Public services, grants and constituency assistance must never depend on how someone voted. Safety is a shared responsibility, not a test of political loyalty.

A public account of the island plan↓

Publish quarterly updates on port and airport work, scholarship access and unresolved service needs. Show what is complete, funded or still proposed. Invite questions across political traditions and report follow-up actions. JB’s role is to negotiate with responsible authorities and scrutinize their delivery, with no claim of personal control over every contract.

Equal citizenship & accountable power↗

The test for every announcement

Proposed is not funded.
Funded is not finished.

JB will seek a quarterly public account that names the responsible authority, separates approved funding from proposals, and shows what people can actually use. No ribbon-cutting should stand in for a working service.

Major investment must pass independent appraisal and fit the Alliance’s fiscal rules. Publish the price of incentives, borrowing and maintenance as clearly as the promised benefits.
