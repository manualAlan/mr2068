01 / Your time

One region. One network.

Your journey should not become more complicated just because it crosses a regional border.

One CATA fare system↓

Negotiate a common ticket and daily fare cap across bus, tram, metro, ferry and regional rail in Montiablo, Reno and Oplana. Keep a cash-accessible option alongside contactless payment. Publish the revenue-sharing agreement so integrated fares do not become an unfunded promise.

Make the connection, not another complaint↓

Start with an audit of missed transfers, overcrowding and first and last services. Coordinate timetables around actual journeys, including shift work. Set public reliability and accessibility standards in competitively awarded operating contracts, with penalties for repeated failures.

Build where the need is greatest↓

Rank proposed rail and interchange upgrades by travel time saved, demand, accessibility and total lifetime cost. Publish the business cases before procurement. Pair station improvements with safe crossings, secure cycle parking and dependable feeder services.

02 / Your bills

Power growth. Protect the bill payer.

A growing capital needs dependable energy. An ambitious project still has to earn the public’s trust.

Put New Homerville under the spotlight↓

Back the proposed 7,000 MW New Homerville project subject to independent safety, financing and cost reviews. Publish construction milestones, contract changes and risks. Capacity is a project ambition, not a claim about electricity already delivered.

No blank check↓

Require staged investment decisions tied to verified progress, transparent procurement and an independent assessment of alternatives. Any public guarantee must disclose the taxpayer’s exposure. Bring material cost overruns back for public scrutiny before additional commitments.

Connect investment to household value↓

Publish a bill-impact assessment covering generation, grid upgrades and financing, rather than promising an arbitrary bill cut. Work with regulators on clear tariffs and fair access for competing suppliers. Support targeted home-efficiency upgrades where they deliver demonstrable savings.

03 / Your next step

Room for your next good idea.

An apartment near work. A first customer. A venue with its lights on. That is what opportunity looks like on a city street.

A single front door for small firms↓

Create one application route for business, premises and routine local permits, with a named case officer, published fees and decision deadlines. Start the clock once an application is complete. Delays should trigger escalation, not automatic approval of unsafe premises.

Homes near the places life happens↓

Use the Alliance’s housing program to bring suitable brownfield sites and underused land near frequent transit into use. Agree water, school and transport capacity before major growth. Publish site-level progress and require clear design and safety standards, with predictable planning decisions.

Keep the capital creative↓

Introduce a coordinated licensing route for small cultural venues and temporary uses of vacant shops. Protect residents through clear noise, access and safety conditions. When new development arrives beside an established venue, require a plan to manage the impact rather than treating closure as the default.

04 / Your say

You should not need an inside contact.

A government promise belongs on the public record, with someone responsible for keeping it.

A dashboard with the difficult numbers too↓

Seek quarterly public reporting on transit reliability, energy-project costs, permit times and neighborhood service requests. Show the baseline, responsible agency, next milestone and explanation for any delay. Publish usable data, not just favorable headlines.

Hear the neighborhood before the decision↓

Hold regular open constituency sessions at accessible locations and publish anonymized themes and follow-up actions. Provide an offline route for residents who cannot use digital services. Protect private casework rather than turning personal difficulties into publicity.

Use the MP’s role honestly↓

An MP cannot personally run the transit system or grant local planning permission. Jon will pursue agreements with the responsible authorities, scrutinize budgets and report how he votes. New spending proposals must identify costs, funding and the institution accountable for delivery.

Meet Jon Fraser

“This city does
not need
a spectator.”

Jon FraserPEOPLE’S PARTY · MONTIABLO

Jon has served as Attorney General of Montiablo for eight years. He is now standing for a capital that works as one connected region, and for public decisions people can actually follow.

His campaign puts practical reform ahead of another list of disconnected projects. Integrate CATA. Scrutinize New Homerville. Make it easier to open a business or a cultural venue. Show residents who is responsible and what happens next.

As the People’s Party candidate within the Liberal-Conservative Alliance, he will argue for Montiablo at the national table while working with neighboring representatives on the issues no region can solve alone.

That means cooperation on transport with Reno and Oplana, national support for housing and enterprise, and a public account of the choices made in your name.
