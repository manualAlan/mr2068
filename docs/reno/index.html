The Reno plan

Keep what makes
life good.
Make room for more.

Saturday at the shops. Dinner with friends. The quiet street you turn into at the end of a long day. Reno offers a life people work hard to build, and good government should help them keep it.

That means a dependable journey to work, neighborhoods cared for properly, and room for the next generation to put down roots. It also means a government that respects your money, your rights and your right to an answer.

Roberto Libero brings a constitutional scholar’s discipline to those everyday expectations. His starting point is simple: public power must serve the people who live with its decisions.

THE JOURNEY HOME IS PART OF YOUR QUALITY OF LIFE.

01 / Your time

Home is the point.
Not the commute.

Reno and Montiablo share a working day. Their transport systems should act like it.

Roberto will work with the Alliance’s neighboring representatives for a common CATA fare system across bus, tram, metro, ferry and regional rail. The proposed daily cap would work with the Alliance’s weekly cap across participating networks. Coordinated transfers and cash-accessible payment would simplify a regional journey without forcing everyone onto an app.

Start with the connections people actually miss. Publish reliability by route, coordinate first and last services for shift workers, and make accessibility part of operating contracts. Fund improvements through a published agreement between the responsible authorities, with clear costs and responsibilities.

02 / Your home

You shouldn’t have
to leave Reno
to start your life.

A first place of your own. A little more space. Somewhere smaller, near the people you love.

Reno’s appeal should not become a closed door. Roberto backs more housing choices on suitable sites near frequent transit and established centers, including smaller homes for people who want to downsize and apartments for those taking their first step.

Growth should come with a plan for school places, water, safe crossings and recreation, agreed before major development goes ahead. Publish the infrastructure funding and construction sequence so existing neighbors can see what will change and new residents are not left waiting for basic services.

Protect established green spaces and give residents a meaningful hearing. Apply the Alliance’s decision clock: 120 days for complete growth-zone applications, or 60 days for compliant infill using approved designs, with safety and environmental safeguards intact. A decision deadline is not automatic permission. It is an end to leaving everyone in limbo.

03 / Your economy

The places you love.
The people who make them work.

Reno’s shops, restaurants and leisure venues are more than a way to spend a weekend. They are employers, first businesses and the places a neighborhood comes together.

Let the next good
idea open its doors.

Enterprise, without favoritism ↗

Roberto will press for a single application route for routine business and premises permits, with one named officer, clear fees and a published decision clock. Inspections should reflect actual risk. Delays should trigger escalation, not automatic permission to open an unsafe venue.

Make it easier to bring empty shops back into use through temporary-use licenses with clear safety and noise conditions. Coordinate deliveries, access and evening transport with local businesses and residents. A thriving center needs customers who can reach it and staff who can get home.

Use the Alliance’s equipment-expensing proposals to help firms invest, and its prompt-payment standard to stop small suppliers financing government’s delays. Support should follow published rules, not a relationship with a minister. Compete for the customer; do not compete for political favors.

THE TEST: CAN PEOPLE ACTUALLY REACH CARE?

The Reno standard · In the neighborhood

Feel safe.
Stay free.
Expect both.

A well-kept park. A safe trip home. Someone who takes a report seriously.

Roberto supports visible neighborhood policing focused on repeat theft, harassment and the places residents identify as unsafe. Police should publish response times and case follow-up standards, alongside an independent route for complaints. Prevention matters too: repair failed lighting, improve crossings and design public spaces with clear sightlines.

Security is not a blank check for surveillance. New monitoring systems should need an evidence-based case, narrow purposes, limits on data retention and independent oversight. The same law must protect the shopper, the business owner and the person stopped on the street.

For local services, residents deserve one case reference and a clear escalation route when a repair goes unanswered. Publish maintenance backlogs and response times, including the difficult figures. A prosperous region still has to get the basics right.

05 / Your government

The rules should
bind the people
who make them.

A CONSTITUTIONAL PRINCIPLE.
AN EVERYDAY GUARANTEE.

When ministers can rewrite the rules without scrutiny, families and businesses cannot plan with confidence. Libero’s defense of Parliament is a defense of your right to know, to question and to be treated fairly.

No permanent
emergency powers.

Require a clear legal basis, a defined expiry date and parliamentary approval for extending emergency measures. Major changes to citizens’ rights belong in legislation, with time for scrutiny, rather than being smuggled through executive orders. Preserve independent courts and access to judicial review.

Your money.
A public account.

Publish the costs, funding and long-term obligations behind major projects before commitments are made. Disclose contract changes and public guarantees, protect independent audit and require ministers to answer substantive committee findings. An impressive announcement is not a substitute for a sound business case.

Conviction without
the closed mind.

Review programs against evidence of results. Keep what works, repair what does not and explain the tradeoffs. Avoid unfunded promises of tax cuts or spending increases. Constitutional restraint and financial discipline should apply to Roberto’s own side just as firmly as to its opponents.

Meet Roberto Libero

A serious mind.
An independent
judgment.

RLRENO · 2068

The son of a defense attorney, Roberto took a more academic path. As a respected professor of constitutional law, he made institutional balance and the limits of executive authority central to his work.

His move into politics grew from frustration with the erosion of parliamentary sovereignty. He believed that decisions affecting the whole country were slipping beyond the scrutiny of its elected representatives, and that defending those institutions required more than writing about them.

Within his party, he became known as a disciplined, technically minded figure. He is distinctly conservative, but practical about how government works: examine the evidence, understand the legal powers, test the cost and listen to people who disagree.

That combination matters in Reno. A community built around homes, enterprise and a high quality of life needs confidence in the rules, careful stewardship of public money and a representative who does the reading before making the promise.

As Moderate Reform’s candidate within the Liberal-Conservative Alliance, Roberto will bring Reno’s priorities to the national program. He will work across regional boundaries where cooperation helps, and scrutinize his own government where scrutiny is needed.

An MP’s job is to legislate, scrutinize and represent. Roberto will seek agreements with the agencies responsible for transport, policing and local services, and publish an account of the votes and decisions he can actually influence.

Representation, with a record

The work should
be open to question.

Roberto will publish a quarterly constituency account: the votes he cast, the agreements he is seeking and the agency responsible when delivery stalls. Residents should see both the progress and the unresolved questions.

Homes, enterprise, infrastructure and services all depend on the same foundation: lawful authority and honest public finances. Reno’s case will be made within the Alliance’s national framework, not through a separate set of unfunded promises.
