WHAT WE BELIEVE

We believe talent deserves a clear route forward.

Good education gives people more choices. The ability to read confidently, work with numbers and question an argument belongs at the heart of it. So does the chance to master a trade, pursue a discovery or learn something new when work changes.

We want clear standards and professional freedom together. Teachers need time to teach. Colleges need equipment and real placements. Universities need independence. Families and learners should be able to see what a course offers, what it costs and where it has led, without mistaking a glossy prospectus for a guarantee.

A practical skill is a serious ambition.

Get the foundations right

Reading, writing and mathematics make later learning possible. Give them protected time, proven materials and regular checks that help teachers act early. Science, civic knowledge and critical thinking belong beside them.

Respect different routes

A technical qualification, an apprenticeship and a university degree should each stand for demanding work. Make the choices understandable and qualifications portable, so one decision does not close every other door.

Show the learning

Report progress, completion and next destinations with context and privacy safeguards. Use evidence to improve teaching and funding. A crude league table cannot explain every school's work or every student's path.

OUR 2068 COMMITMENTS

What opportunity needs

A practical plan, with standards to meet and choices made in public.

Help early. Teach the essentials.+

Offer a language and development check two years before school, followed by timely support. Protect daily reading, writing and mathematics with clear national standards and room for teacher judgment. Use regular checks to guide help, not simply record that a learner has fallen behind.

Give teachers time and classrooms focus+

Cut duplicate reporting, provide quality lesson resources and create senior careers for expert classroom teachers. Set clear behavior expectations and keep phones out of instructional time, with health and disability exceptions. Give every additional needs support plan one coordinator and a legal deadline.

Technical training connected to real work+

Agree regional college programs with employers in construction, maritime engineering, health, energy, manufacturing and cybersecurity. Fund equipment and placements through open agreements. Direct genuinely unused training levy balances into accredited apprenticeships and portable accounts, verifying training and completion rather than relabeling existing jobs.

Know the course before you commit+

Publish teaching hours, completion, employment, earnings ranges and likely debt before enrollment. Explain academic, technical and apprenticeship routes on a consistent basis. Recognize prior learning, and report next destinations with context: a past earnings figure informs a choice but cannot promise a salary.

Keep discovery free, make invention useful+

Protect university autonomy, academic freedom and independent peer review. Introduce standard research licenses and testing awards with fixed end dates. Publish ownership and returns from publicly supported discoveries. Evaluate additional investment and results before expanding a scheme.

THE DELIVERY TEST

Judge the promise by the result.

What happens first, what is measured, and how it is paid for.

First step
By 2069, establish core standards and legal deadlines for support plans. By 2070, regional technical agreements and standard research licenses.
Public test
Annual school and destination reports, course completion and employment data, and published results from research commercialization.
Funding rule
Verify unused levy balances and existing obligations before redirecting funds. Publish equipment and placement agreements; evaluate awards before expansion.
