WHAT WE BELIEVE

We believe prosperity grows when people have the freedom to try something of their own.

The next good employer might be a bakery opening a second shop, an engineer starting a workshop or a researcher finding a use for a discovery. Government cannot know every idea worth trying. It can keep the rules fair, make failure survivable and leave people room to make their own decisions.

We are for enterprise and the competition that keeps it honest. A market works when a newcomer can challenge an established firm and customers can take their business elsewhere. Rewarding work, paying bills on time and managing public money carefully give that ambition a dependable foundation.

A good idea should not need a political connection.

Let effort take you further

An extra shift, a promotion or a return to work should bring a clear reward. People deserve to understand what they will keep and make their own choices about earning, saving and supporting the people they love.

Keep the market open

Being for business means making room for businesses that do not exist yet. Fair competition, easy switching and open public contracts give newcomers a chance. Political favor should never be worth more than a better product or service.

Make promises we can afford

Sound finances make it possible to plan and to respond when a crisis arrives. Permanent commitments need permanent funding. People should see the costs and the choices, including what government will change when a promise proves too expensive.

OUR 2068 COMMITMENTS

The practical part

A practical plan, with standards to meet and choices made in public.

Keep the reward for work+

Retain the 20% earnings credit on the first 20,000 aurums of the reference income band, worth up to 4,000 aurums before other credits and indexation. Keep tax thresholds indexed to inflation and publish a calculator showing tax and benefit withdrawals together, so earning more has a clear meaning.

Give small firms a fair start+

Process complete standard business registrations within one day, with a named contact and a staffed alternative. Require public bodies to pay undisputed small business invoices within 20 working days, with automatic interest for delays. Divide suitable contracts into smaller lots and publish who wins them and why.

Investment and skills that travel together+

Allow immediate expensing of qualifying machinery, software and clean equipment. Keep the 40% research and human capital credit for audited additional spending, not relabeled routine costs. Use genuinely unused training levy balances for accredited apprenticeships and portable training accounts, with published completion and employment results.

Make it easier to choose a better competitor+

Strengthen competition investigations and interim remedies, subject to appeal. Challenge state favoritism and exclusionary platforms. Clear charges, easy switching and portable data should let customers take their business elsewhere. Open public contracts to smaller firms and disclose owners, selection reasons and changes.

Keep debt moving down+

Aim to reduce gross public debt from 68% of GDP in 2068 to 65% by 2072 under normal conditions, while retaining the separate net debt ceiling below 70%. Treasury must publish a correction plan within 30 days when a budget departs from the certified path. Parliament sets the pace, avoiding automatic cuts that deepen a downturn.

THE DELIVERY TEST

Judge the promise by the result.

What happens first, what is measured, and how it is paid for.

First step
In the first budget, publish indexed tax schedules, the tax and benefit calculator and an independently tested fiscal path.
Public test
By 2069, standard incorporation in one day and public small-business invoices paid within 20 working days. Report competition and contract access annually.
Funding rule
Pay for recurring commitments with recurring revenue or verified permanent savings. Publish ten-year costs; do not spend asset sales or hoped-for growth on permanent promises.
