Healthcare

Someone should answer.

When someone you love is ill, knowing what happens next should be the easy part.

↓

WHAT WE BELIEVE

We believe everyone deserves care they can reach and people they can trust.

Illness makes the world smaller. Work is rearranged, plans are canceled and a family waits for a call. A decent health service understands that time matters. People need an appointment they can book, an explanation they can understand and a person who knows what comes next.

Universal access is a responsibility we accept. We also believe patients deserve choice and clinicians deserve room to use their judgment. Every qualified service that can provide safe, effective care has something to offer. We will judge the system by what happens to the person who needs it.

Care should leave you feeling less alone.

Your need comes first

The care you receive should follow your clinical need. Income, address and confidence in navigating the system should not decide whether you get help. Universal coverage must mean a practical route to treatment for everyone.

Trust the people doing the work

Nurses, pharmacists and doctors should be able to use the skills they trained for. Clear standards, proper staffing and less repeated administration give them more time to listen, make decisions and look after their patients.

Give patients a say

You deserve to understand your choices, see your own record and know who is responsible for your care. Services should listen when something goes wrong, explain it honestly and make the next step easier to find.

OUR 2068 COMMITMENTS

Making care easier to reach

A practical plan, with standards to meet and choices made in public.

An appointment within reach+

Set same-day access for urgent primary care and appointments within seven days for routine needs, phased with verified staffing. Expand safe roles for nurses and pharmacists. District waiting figures must include people unable to book, so the full demand for care remains visible.

A way through the waiting list+

When a service misses a clinically defined maximum elective wait, its commissioner must arrange suitable alternative care at no extra charge. Confirm capacity before the guarantee begins. Public and independent providers will meet the same quality rules, including safeguards for people whose treatment is complex.

Mental health without the maze+

Provide one assessment pathway and a named navigator. By 2070, urgent nonemergency referrals for people under 25 should receive first clinical contact within 14 days. Emergencies need immediate assessment. Publish subsequent treatment waits too, because a first conversation must lead to the right care.

One standard, whoever provides the care+

Publish common measures of safety, infections, mortality and patient experience across public and independent services. Give an independent inspector powers for unannounced visits and enforceable improvements. Contracts must reward quality and continuity, with safeguards against avoiding people whose care is complex or costly.

Fund the service before promising the saving+

Use budgets covering several years to build primary, community and elective capacity. Publish staffing and referral agreements before activating guarantees. Keep existing provision until replacements work. Prevention can improve lives and future costs, but hoped-for savings cannot pay today's wages.

THE DELIVERY TEST

Judge the promise by the result.

What happens first, what is measured, and how it is paid for.

First step
Publish funded district access plans in 2069, with staffing and referral capacity verified before guarantees begin.
Public test
Report actual appointment and treatment waits, including people unable to book. Publish safety and patient experience on the same basis for every provider.
Funding rule
Show costs over ten years before budget votes. Commission capacity within a multiyear budget and count savings only when verified.
