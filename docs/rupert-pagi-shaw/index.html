The case I am making

Growth should leave a place stronger than it found it.

In Kalahooska, stewardship and enterprise belong together. A well-managed forest can support skilled work and local businesses while renewing the resource they depend on. I want government to bring that same long view to roads, homes and public services.

A new mill is only part of the story. Its workers need somewhere to live, its suppliers need a dependable road and the surrounding towns need schools and care they can count on. My plan connects those needs, with more practical decisions made close to the people who live with them.

People should be free to build, invest and make a living. Government’s job is to keep the rules fair, the connections working and the public accounts open. That is the kind of growth I will argue for in Parliament.

01 / Homes & independence

Room to put down roots.

A good job and a place to live should be part of the same conversation. Make room in our towns, with the services a growing community needs.

The national commitment ↗
Service the land. Then build.+

Seek a regional growth compact that identifies suitable infill, water capacity, schools and transport together. Publish sites and the next decision for each, with funded maintenance. Kalahooska should contribute to the Alliance’s goal of 50% more annual home completions by 2072, against the verified 2068 total, while respecting flood safety and valuable habitat.

A decision people can plan around+

Apply the national 120-day clock to complete growth-zone applications and 60 days to compliant infill using approved designs. Give smaller builders a fair route to bid. A missed deadline should bring a fee refund and an independent appeal, never permission to bypass safety.

A dependable rental. A careful route to ownership.+

Back lasting rental contracts, portable deposits and enforceable repair standards. Reserve capped shared ownership support for eligible households buying additional new homes, with clear income limits and resale terms. Help should create a home, not simply inflate the price of an existing one.

02 / Working forests

More value from every tree.

Our natural wealth should support skilled work and long-term stewardship close to home.

The national commitment ↗
Turn more timber into finished products+

Support open investment in engineered timber, furniture, useful byproducts and modern mills where demand and sustainable supply support a business case. Connect firms to the Alliance’s equipment expensing and export certification proposals, with competition deciding who earns the order.

Harvest, restore, repeat+

Set transparent regional forest plans based on independent inventories. Require replanting, habitat protection and funded restoration, with results published by catchment and concession.

Open contracts to local firms+

Publish forestry, road and public building pipelines early. Break suitable contracts into accessible lots and enforce prompt payment without weakening safety or value tests.

03 / Connections

Distance should not decide your future.

A failed bridge or unreliable signal can isolate a family, a clinic and a business at once.

The national commitment ↗
Keep essential roads open+

Publish a condition audit of bridges, slopes and freight routes, then fund maintenance by safety and isolation risk. Coordinate heavy freight with road capacity and repair plans. Show the responsible agency, disruption arrangements and next milestone.

Reliable digital access+

Tender support only where the market cannot provide service, paying for tested speed and reliability. Give clinics and essential services backup connections. Keep telephone and in-person access to public services.

Transport built around real journeys+

Coordinate regional services around work, school and medical appointments. Use competitive essential-route contracts where demand cannot sustain a commercial service, with public reliability standards and clear disruption information.

04 / Skills & public services

A future here needs more than a job.

A training place you can reach. A clinic that answers. Good schools and clean water. These are the foundations of a town people choose to call home.

The national commitment ↗
Train for the work being built+

Agree paid pathways in forestry, engineering, care and construction with employers and colleges. Share apprenticeship administration so small firms can take part. Publish course completion and next destinations, and verify equipment, placements and funding before promising additional places.

Care with a clear way in+

Join up primary care, visiting clinics and patient transport. Phase the national same-day urgent primary-care standard and seven-day routine appointment standard with verified staffing. Publish waits that include people unable to book, and a contact for stalled referrals. Back the national funded respite offer for unpaid caregivers.

Make the basics visible+

Report water tests and repair schedules by service area. Support strong reading and mathematics standards, with room for teachers to teach. Public reporting should explain the funding, the progress and the support still needed, while protecting individual records.

05 / Local power

Decisions closer to the people living with them.

Regional authority must come with the revenue and responsibility to make it real.

The national commitment ↗
A practical regional settlement+

Seek lasting regional powers over local transport, skills and land management, backed by multi-year budgets. Set funding responsibilities in law and retain independent audit. Residents should know which authority can act and which account pays.

Keep project value here+

Return a transparent share of royalties from nationally significant resource projects to host communities for durable infrastructure and skills. Publish receipts and their use, and avoid making essential services depend on a single volatile revenue source.

Open government by default+

Publish contracts, land decisions and delivery dashboards in usable form, with assisted access for residents who are offline. Protect personal casework and an independent route to challenge a public decision.

The everyday test

The last mile
is someone’s home.

A road is a school journey, a delivery route and the way to a medical appointment. Maintenance deserves the same attention as a new project.

I will seek a published condition audit and a funded repair schedule, starting with safety and the risk of communities being cut off. Residents should be able to see the responsible authority and the next milestone.

Local power.
Public answers.

An MP cannot sign every road contract or run every clinic. Rupert’s role would be to secure agreements, scrutinize spending and bring unresolved local problems into Parliament.

THE FIRST REQUEST

Put the decisions in one place.

Seek a regional compact listing the housing sites, service capacity, forest plans and essential connections needing decisions. Each entry should name its authority and funding source.

THE REGULAR ACCOUNT

Report progress each quarter.

Separate proposals from funded work and completed projects. Publish delays and the next action, not just a list of announcements. Protect private constituent casework.

THE NONNEGOTIABLE RULE

No favored firms. No hidden bill.

Open contracts, independent audit and usable appeals. Resource revenues should build lasting assets; essential services need dependable funding, not a wager on next year’s receipts.
